# loxodonta and the security controls a reviewer checks

This page is a mapping, never an attestation: no row says whether a deployment achieves a control, which is decided by that deployment and its assessor.

Each row names a control, the loxodonta mechanism that bears on it, the relationship between the two, and what is left to the deployment. On most rows the last column is where the control is decided.

## How to read a row

The relationships are those of the supportive relationship mapping in [NIST IR 8477](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8477/final), section 4.2. A mechanism *Supports* a control when it "can be applied alone or in combination with one or more other concepts" to achieve the control "in whole or in part", and every row here adds the property *Example of*: the mechanism is one way of achieving the control, and the control can be achieved without it. *Integral to*, the property for a mechanism a control cannot be achieved without, is on no row, because every control below can be achieved with other tools.

IR 8477 reads every relationship within a stated use case. This page's is a review of an environment where an AI agent writes the log a control is about, and where that agent is the adversary (ADR-0002). There are no strength scores and no marks: a row says what the mechanism does and where it stops.

## The mapping

| **NIST SP 800-53 AU-5**, Response to Audit Logging Process Failures: alert named people within a set time "in the event of an audit logging process failure" | The completeness alarm: the supervisor's [witness](GLOSSARY.md#witness) counts the tool calls a session's [second record](GLOSSARY.md#second-record) says owed a receipt, and `supervisor scan` exits 6 when a live session's receipts stop arriving (ADR-0034) | Supports, *Example of* | Who is alerted, and how fast: the exit code and the [`/metrics`](METRICS.md) route reach a person only through the cron job or pager the deployment wires, and a failure is seen only as often as a scan runs. AU-5's further actions: loxodonta takes none and never stops the agent. The witness reads Claude Code's transcript only, a record the writer can reach, so it catches a disabled hook and not a writer shaping both records. |
| **NIST SP 800-53 AU-9(2)**, Store on Separate Physical Systems or Components: store the records "in a repository that is part of a physically different system or system component than the system or component being audited" | The [published chain](GLOSSARY.md#published-chain), sent to the [receiver](RECEIVER.md), which appends what arrives and has no route to delete it (ADR-0031) | Supports, *Example of* | Whether the receiver's box is physically separate and out of the writer's reach: no SSH key, shared folder or browser login reaches it from the agent's machine ([RECEIVER.md](RECEIVER.md) section 8). The frequency: entries leave at session end and on the keeper's cadence, six hours by default, so a busy session's tail stays on the machine until it ends. The `full` profile, without which nothing leaves. Retention, and whoever runs the receiver, who can delete its files. |

Whether the receiver is secure and internal. Every log in scope besides the receipts, which loxodonta does not carry. |
| **PCI DSS v4 10.3.4**, paraphrased: change detection on the logs, so that existing log data cannot change without an alert | The [hash chain](SPEC.md) and `verify`, run over the whole store by `supervisor scan`, which exits 1 to 4 for the worst verdict and 5 when the [baseline](GLOSSARY.md#baseline) sees a change appends cannot explain | Supports, *Example of* | The alert, as for AU-5. What the machine cannot settle: an edit breaks the chain, but a chain regenerated whole, rewritten consistently from some entry on, or cut at its tail walks clean, and the baseline that would notice is a file the writer can rewrite ([TOPOLOGY.md](TOPOLOGY.md)). The next row is for that. |
| **PCI DSS v4 10.3.4**, as above | A commitment held off the machine: the [anchor](ANCHORING.md), through OpenTimestamps into Bitcoin (ADR-0003), and the [published head](GLOSSARY.md#published-head) (ADR-0025) | Supports, *Example of* | Only the part of a chain a commitment reached is covered; the entries after it can be rewritten. Nothing compares on its own: the check is `verify --anchors` with a block header from a source the checker trusts, or `verify --expect-head` against the heads the remote holds, run by someone on some schedule. Who holds the published heads, and the `timestamped` or `full` profile that sends them. |
| **PCI DSS v4 10.7.2**, paraphrased: failures of critical security control systems, the logging mechanisms among them, are detected, alerted and addressed promptly | The completeness alarm, as for AU-5 | Supports, *Example of* | Everything AU-5's row leaves, and the response: loxodonta reports a failure and addresses none. It bears on the logging item only, among the many systems the requirement lists. |
| **OWASP Agentic AI Threats and Mitigations, T8**, Repudiation and Untraceability: its mitigation asks for comprehensive logging and "cryptographic verification", with logs signed and protected from change | The hash chain, with a commitment held off the machine: the anchor, the published head, the published chain | Supports, *Example of* | Who acted: a receipt does not say who wrote it, and its `actor` names a harness, as [testimony](GLOSSARY.md#testimony). The signing: the writer signs nothing, because the signer would be the adversary (ADR-0001). Where the commitments are held, as in the rows above. |
| **OWASP T8**, as above | The [transcript commitment](GLOSSARY.md#transcript-commitment): the chain fingerprints the harness transcript every 25 receipts, so the record of why an action was taken is committed by reference (ADR-0017) | Supports, *Example of* | Keeping the transcript: the harness deletes it on a retention cycle, and only a package made with `--transcript` carries it. The window before each commitment, in which the writer can rewrite the transcript freely; a forgery written before the first commitment is committed faithfully. |
| **AIUC-1 E015.4**, Log integrity protection, paraphrased: technical controls make logs tamper-evident and independently verifiable | The [package](PACKAGE.md) and `verify-package`: a session's chains, sidecars and manifest, checked by the recipient with one file, `verifier.py`, copied out of the recorder (ADR-0026, ADR-0035) | Supports, *Example of* | Which sessions are packaged, and when. How the recipient gets the verifier, and the issuer's key fingerprint through a channel the package cannot rewrite. The seals chosen: an unsealed package verifies at most `SELF-CONSISTENT`, which a regenerated one also reaches. A package says it is unaltered since packaging, never that its contents are true or complete. |

The fields the draft lists that a receipt does not carry, among them an authenticated agent identifier, the delegated subject and the authorization decision: those come from the deployment's other records. |

## Considered, no row

- **NIST SP 800-53 AU-9(4)** limits the management of logging to a subset of privileged users. loxodonta restricts no one: the writer can edit its own settings and remove the hook. The risk the control's discussion names, a privileged user who is also audited "inhibiting audit activities or modifying audit records", is the one this project starts from (ADR-0002), and loxodonta's answer to it is detection, in the AU-5 and 10.3.4 rows, and not the restriction the control asks for.
- **PCI DSS v4 10.3.1** limits read access to logs to those with a job-related need. The chains are plain files any local process can read.
- **PCI DSS v4 10.3.2** protects logs against modification. loxodonta protects nothing against modification; it makes modification show, which is the 10.3.4 rows.

## Where the frameworks stop

Each of these controls pictures a human administrator as the party who might stop a log or alter it. Here that party is the agent writing it, a case none of them contemplates ([GROUNDING.md](GROUNDING.md) section 4). What the design can and cannot claim is [GROUNDING.md](GROUNDING.md) section 2: detection, never prevention; tamper-evidence only for the part of a chain a commitment held off the machine covers; and never who wrote it.
